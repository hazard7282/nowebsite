Miami Minimal & Deep Tech Scene

A working DJ's guide to the stripped-back side of Miami house music — minimal, deep tech, rominimal, the people and parties around it, and where to hear the sound online.

By Adam Bogdan · Miami, Florida · Updated August 2026

Miami is usually sold through the loudest parts of its electronic music culture. Under that surface is a smaller, groove-first lane built around minimal house, deep tech, rominimal and stripped-back underground house. It lives through local selectors, label nights, radio sessions, after-hours rooms and visiting European artists rather than one single venue or promoter.

I'm Adam Bogdan, a Romanian-born DJ and producer based in Miami and the founder of Rapture's Deli. This page is not meant to rank every DJ in the city. It is a local map of the part of Miami's scene I actually work in: the slower-burn, groove-heavy corner where Romanian minimal, deep tech and house overlap.

What minimal and deep tech mean here

The labels overlap. In a Miami set, a night can move from dubby minimal into rolling deep tech, then into a warmer house record without anybody stopping to rename the genre. The common thread is usually space, repetition, low-end movement and a long-form DJ approach rather than constant drops.

Minimal House

Reduced arrangements, tight percussion and small changes that become important over longer blends.

Deep Tech

More low-end pressure and club drive, while keeping the arrangement restrained and groove-led.

Rominimal

The Romanian-influenced end of minimal: hypnotic, detailed and patient, with micro-rhythms and long transitions at the center.

The Romanian minimal connection in Miami

For me, the connection is literal. I started DJing in Romania before moving to Miami, so the Romanian minimal vocabulary is part of my musical background rather than a later discovery. In Miami, that vocabulary sits naturally beside deep tech and underground house: the room still wants groove, but it does not need every record to announce itself immediately.

The crossover has also made Miami a natural home for minimal house music and deep tech music in my life, especially in smaller underground rooms where groove and long-form DJ programming matter more than big-room drops.

That same crossover is also visible in Miami lineups. Resident Advisor has documented local events with Romanian artists including Barac, Lizz, Suciu and Sublee, alongside Miami-based residents and selectors. Adam Bogdan's RA profile also lists Miami among his most-played regions and MODE Downtown Miami, The Sylvester, Mezcalista and Fontainebleau among his most-played local venues.

The useful distinction: rominimal is part of this page because it is one of the roots feeding Miami's stripped-back house circuit. It is not a claim that every Miami minimal night is a Romanian-minimal night.

Rapture's Deli: label, events and local platform

Rapture's Deli is the platform I built around this sound in Miami. It operates as both an event series and an independent label, connecting local DJs with visiting underground artists and releasing music across deep tech, minimal house, rominimal and adjacent house styles.

The event archive on Resident Advisor includes Rapture's Deli nights with artists such as Suciu and Sublee, as well as Miami lineups at venues including The Sylvester, MODE Downtown Miami and Mezcalista. The label side has released music from Adam Bogdan, Sepp and Sublee, with releases available through Beatport and Bandcamp.

Where to follow the Miami underground without the hype

For this corner of the scene, I would follow artists, labels and event series rather than relying only on broad “Miami nightlife” listings. Resident Advisor is useful for checking lineups and archives; YouTube and SoundCloud are better for hearing what selectors actually play; Beatport and Bandcamp are where the releases and labels become easier to trace.

For a deeper breakdown of the sound itself, read What Is Rominimal? Romanian Minimal Explained.

For my side of it, the shortest route is: YouTube for sets, Beatport and Bandcamp for releases, Spotify for streaming, and Rapture's Deli for the local label/event connection.
